Gate 1: is a learned surrogate of the collision integral needed?

The plan's test has two parts. The exact, momentum-resolved integral must cost at least 100 times more per evaluation than the fast FFT-based integral, and the fast one must miss the exact permittivity transient by more than 2 %. Both hold at 512 energy points. At the resolution the transients actually need, only the second holds.

The exact integral scales with the cube of the grid size, and the FFT integral barely grows. A film run of the plan's size, 100 cells and a million evaluations, takes about 28 minutes at 128 points on this 4-core machine, 3.5 hours at 256 and 22 hours at 512. The plan assumed 1 to 10 days.

  • The FFT integral fails mainly through its collision rate, which sets the optical damping and grows with the cube of the density of states. Its error on the distribution alone is 3–11 % at equal absorbed energy.
  • Anything that replaces the exact integral must reproduce the collision rate as well as the collision term.
  • The thermal model built from the same operators is the best cheap model: 3–17 % on Re ε, worst for 20 fs pumps and 3–5 % at 220 fs. That residual is the non-thermal contribution hypothesis H1 is about.
  • The plan's fallback for a failed gate, using the FFT integral inside the film solver, should not be taken.

What agrees

  • Absorptivity within 1–6 % at all six intensities.
  • Peak electron temperature within 2–7 % up to 22 GW/cm², and for the weaker 30 fs case.
  • With a 6 eV band the three highest 220 fs cases come within 5 % (6203, 8777 and 10771 K against 5902, 8393 and 10262 K).

What does not, and why

  • With the stated 4 eV band the peak temperature overshoots by 16–32 % at 50–75 GW/cm², because the band saturates.
  • The change of Re ε is 20–28 % smaller than published. The paper's plotted permittivity implies a plasma frequency 8 % above what its printed parameters give.
  • Im ε matches to 1–6 % only if the screening wavevector is frozen at its 300 K value; the paper's equation defines it from the hot distribution.

Two things the paper does not print had to be inferred: the pump arrives at 60° in air and refracts through the glass substrate, and the conduction band is wider than 4 eV. Closing the remaining gaps needs the authors' unprinted parameters.

The solver

What it does

  • Evolves the electron distribution f(E, t) in a non-parabolic band together with the lattice temperature.
  • Electron–electron collisions at two levels: the exact screened-Coulomb integral and the FFT random-k integral.
  • Electron–phonon collisions with momentum conservation, photo-excitation, and switchable Auger-like and two-photon channels.
  • Plasma frequency, damping and permittivity computed from f with no temperature assumed.
  • A thin-film drive that solves the field self-consistently, and a two-temperature baseline built from the same operators.

What the literature changes in the plan

The summary file has a synthesis and one digest per paper. Fifty-two papers were read in full text; seven paywalled ones are reconstructions from abstracts and citing papers and are flagged as such.

  1. Two-photon absorption is closed at 1.1–1.6 µm. With the 2.67 eV gap of reference [23] it needs a pump below 929 nm. The Auger-versus-two-photon test can only run on 800 nm few-cycle data, which the plan defers to Tier 2.
  2. Hypothesis H2 needs rewording. A fixed-density thermal model already reverses the sign of the transmittance [24]. The hypothesis has to be stated for Re ε or the plasma frequency.
  3. The thermal model M0 needs a temperature-dependent damping law with free parameters to produce any reversal.
  4. The density-versus-mass reading of the current equation is the project's own. References [30, 31] give the two mathematical forms but do not make that identification.
  5. The novelty claim survives but must be reworded. Reference [47] already enforces one conservation law exactly, and [49] learns a flow map, not a collision operator. No paper treats Pauli blocking.
  6. "Train at 256 points, evaluate at 512" has no support in the cited neural-operator papers.
  7. References [58–60] are not precedents for double-blind FROG with a complex gate; the closest is the retrieval in [22].

Twenty-six citations in the plan also need updating, mostly preprints that have since been published. Reference [16] has three authors and [22] was retitled.

Dataset pilot and a validity limit

The dataset generator produced eight trajectories with the exact integral at 128 points, about 40 seconds and 4 MB each. Every trajectory stores the distribution, the collision terms the operator would learn, and perturbed off-trajectory states.

A third of the plan's sampling range does not fit in a single-band model. Drawing from 20–250 fs and 0.1 GW/cm² to 10 TW/cm² in-film, 34 % of cases would deposit more than half of what a 4 eV band can hold. The generator now screens those out. The upper intensity range needs higher bands in the model, or an explicit validity limit near 10¹¹ W/cm² in-film for a 100 fs pulse.
